# OpenAI Cuts Off Cursor: 5% Is a Dashboard Light, Not an Escape Map

It went out as a short post in late August, the kind you read on a phone. **OpenAI** was ending its partnership with **Cursor**, the AI coding tool now owned by SpaceX [9](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2093515564786540695). Direct access to OpenAI models ends on **November 12** [9](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2093515564786540695)[7](https://www.ithome.com/0/995/874.htm).

Two numbers now define the split. OpenAI's stated reason was trust, aimed at Elon Musk's record of breaking contracts [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts). Cursor's answer: OpenAI models are about **5%** of its AI traffic [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts).

One number is a verdict. The other is a reassurance. Neither maps what developers actually depend on.

The mechanics came first. On **August 28**, OpenAI formally notified SpaceX that it planned to wind down the contract supplying OpenAI models to Cursor [5](https://openai.com/index/our-decision-on-cursor-following-its-acquisition-by-spacex)[7](https://www.ithome.com/0/995/874.htm)[10](https://aihot.virxact.com/story/87bc9722-9d01-4fb3-a9da-a104ecea99ed). Its X account published the proposal the same day [9](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2093515564786540695).

The company's blog met the moment with finality. Its announcement was framed as a decision, not a negotiation [5](https://openai.com/index/our-decision-on-cursor-following-its-acquisition-by-spacex).

The end date is **November 12, 2026** [7](https://www.ithome.com/0/995/874.htm)[10](https://aihot.virxact.com/story/87bc9722-9d01-4fb3-a9da-a104ecea99ed). Between now and then, nothing disappears from the editor.

It is a proposal, not a finality. OpenAI's own post said 'under our proposal,' leaving a two-and-a-half-month window for the terms to move [9](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2093515564786540695). That is real runway, if anyone uses it.

OpenAI itself named the people in the middle, in the first lines of its post: developers who rely on OpenAI models inside Cursor [9](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2093515564786540695).

The trigger was contractual. One observer read it immediately: OpenAI is invoking the change-of-control provision in Cursor's contract to end model access [6](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2093538312401322387). The clause is common enough in supply agreements to go unnoticed — until ownership moves [6](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2093538312401322387).

*In the original agreement, this was a paragraph. On August 28, it became a headline.*

*Fine print is just a paragraph — until ownership moves, and it becomes the headline.*


The stated reason was thinner than the machinery. The Decoder reported OpenAI citing Musk's history of breaking contracts [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts). One voice in the conversation put it in raw form:

> We unfortunately have decided that we cannot continue providing access to our models through Cursor and are ending our partnership. It boils down to trust and we've asked that this takes effect on November 12 to give you some time to plan.
>
> — @thsottiaux, on X [8](https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2093515916076343774)

Trust is a category, not a technical complaint. Nowhere in the public record is the objection about code quality. It is about who owns the pipe.

Trust, said this way, is a political word. The cited history — Musk's companies breaking contracts — is years of bad blood condensed into one clause [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts). Framed as compliance, the decision is harder to litigate and harder to answer [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts).

Then came the other number. Cursor co-founder Michael Truell moved fast to shrink the story [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts).

OpenAI models, he said, account for about **5%** of Cursor's AI traffic [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts). Relays echoed the figure, second-hand, within hours [3](https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2093598203916468500).

It is a self-reported number. No third-party audit sits behind it, and the public record shows no OpenAI-side figure to check it against [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts).

The word 'about' carries weight in that sentence. So does 'AI traffic' [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts). A percentage without a visible denominator is a mood: it gives you the direction of the impact, never the shape of it.

Five percent of requests can still sit under your most-used workflow — the completion you never think about until it stops. Traffic share and dependency are different things.

*Traffic counts the requests. Dependency counts the workflows that break when the requests stop.*


So what does 5% actually answer? How much of Cursor's volume rides on OpenAI today [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts). What it does not answer: which teams, which features, which daily habits sit behind it.

OpenAI's own post admitted the gap. It named developers as the people most affected, and said nothing about which workflows would change [9](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2093515564786540695).

The notice touches one line: direct access to OpenAI models [9](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2093515564786540695). Nothing in the announcement reaches further than that. What OpenAI didn't say is its own story: no performance claim, no replacement plan, no list of affected features [9](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2093515564786540695)[1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts).

None of this promises a broken editor on November 13. It promises a different one.

Which is why the silence matters. The affected features, the migration plan, the replacement supply — none of it has a public answer yet [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts)[9](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2093515564786540695). For a team that runs Cursor all day, the question is practical [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts).

Where does the model call go after the date — and does the answer change cost, latency, or output? No migration list has been published; the December model menu is still unannounced [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts)[9](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2093515564786540695).

Every Cursor shop now holds the same question. Which of our workflows call an OpenAI model through the built-in path? The answer lives in logs, not announcements.

There is a charitable reading. Maybe 5% is honest, the transition is smooth, and by December nobody remembers the switch [1](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts). Maybe.

The other reading is colder. The clause existed before the acquisition; OpenAI simply chose to use it now [6](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2093538312401322387).

Read it as a contract story, and the lesson comes cheap. If a change-of-control clause can end a supply line like this one, the same fine print sits in other contracts [6](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2093538312401322387). It stays quiet until ownership moves.

Two audiences read the same announcement differently. Investors heard 5% and exhaled. Developers heard a date and started counting workflows.

![Two rooms divided by a wall: on the left an investor exhales in relief beside a phone showing 5%, on the right a developer counts sticky notes on a workflow board under a desk lamp.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sztimhdd/AI_Daily/main/outputs/2026/09/01/openai-cuts-off-cursor-5-is-a-dashboard-light-not-an-escape-map/images/03.webp)
*The same announcement, two different heart rates.*


The market got its number. The developers got a date.

![A paper calendar open to November with the 12th circled twice in red, next to a torn slip of paper printed with 5%.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sztimhdd/AI_Daily/main/outputs/2026/09/01/openai-cuts-off-cursor-5-is-a-dashboard-light-not-an-escape-map/images/04.webp)
*The market got a number to price. The developers got a date to plan around.*


**5%** is a dashboard light.

It is not an escape map.

It says the tank is mostly full. It doesn't say where the road ends.
